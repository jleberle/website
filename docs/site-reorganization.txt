--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. VERDICT UP FRONT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are NOT badly siloed. The instinct that quotes/reviews are "defined" into
boxes is understandable, but the machinery that de-silos them already exists and
is already adopted: the sources taxonomy, the merged Writing archive, shared-tag
relations, research paths, and the "At the desk" homepage band.

The gap between what you have and "an open personal knowledge database" is not
that quotes and reviews are separate sections. It's three things:

  (A) NO TOPIC MAP. The knowledge base has no browsable "what is this collection
      ABOUT" surface. Tags exist but are thin, flat, and auto-only; research
      paths are curated but few and linear. A knowledge base needs standing
      subject hubs. This is the single highest-leverage change.

  (B) THE NOTE MODEL IS TOO NARROW. "Quotes" captures only verbatim passages. A
      knowledge base also wants your own short notes, marginalia, and connective
      observations. Right now those have nowhere to live except a full article.

  (C) NO SINGLE FRONT DOOR TO THE GRAPH. Reading, Notebook, Research Paths, and
      Tags are four separate doors into the same knowledge graph. Nothing ties
      them into one "here is the workshop" entry point.

So: keep the sections. Do NOT collapse quotes/reviews into articles. Instead,
raise the connective layer (topics), broaden the note model, and give the graph
a front door. That's evolution of a system that's already well-designed, not a
teardown.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE REAL TENSION YOU'RE NAVIGATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two goals pull the IA in opposite directions:

  PROFESSIONAL IDENTITY wants:            KNOWLEDGE DATABASE wants:
    - few, stable, legible sections         - dense interlinking
    - clean categories a hiring/tenure      - low-friction capture
      /peer reader can scan                 - many small nodes
    - "output" framing (articles,           - "thinking in public" framing
      reviews, CV, teaching)                - topics over chronology
    - curation                              - accretion

The mistake would be to let one win. If the knowledge base wins, the front page
reads like a personal wiki and the professional signal (historian, lecturer,
peer-reviewed-adjacent output) gets buried. If professional identity wins, the
knowledge work stays locked in publication silos and never becomes a browsable
web of ideas.

The resolution is a TWO-LAYER model (§5): a curated professional surface sitting
on top of an openly-linked knowledge graph, with the sources taxonomy as the
spine connecting both. You already have most of the pieces; they just aren't
named or framed as two coherent layers yet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHERE SILOING ACTUALLY HAPPENS — AND WHERE IT DOESN'T
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT siloed (leave alone):
  - Browsing writing: /archives/ already merges the three writing types.
  - Book <-> writing: a source page already aggregates its review + quotes +
    articles. A book IS a hub already.
  - Related-by-tag footer works across sections.

Genuinely siloed / thin:
  - TOPICS. There is no page that says "everything I have on 1990s right-wing
    movements" (article + review + quotes + sources), with framing. Tag pages
    are the closest thing and they're bare lists.
  - NOTEBOOK is a dead-end island. A quote links OUT to its source, and shows up
    on that source page, but the Notebook itself is just a reverse-chron list. It
    isn't grouped by book or topic, so it reads as a blog of quotes rather than
    as the raw material of a commonplace book.
  - SOURCES are reachable but not browsable AS a graph. /sources/ is suppressed
    (correct call — it duplicated /reading/), so the richest node type only
    surfaces through /reading/ (activity-ordered) or inline links. There is no
    "by subject" view of the library.
  - RESEARCH PATHS are excellent but invisible-by-default (excluded from feed and
    archive; reachable via one link on /archives/). For a knowledge base they
    should be a more prominent, standing feature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PROPOSED TARGET STRUCTURE — THE TWO-LAYER MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Think of the site as two layers over one spine.

  LAYER 1 — PROFESSIONAL SURFACE (curated, stable, few doors)
    /            home: identity + selected work + "at the desk" teaser (as now)
    /about/      who you are
    /cv/         cv + PDF
    /courses/    teaching
    /archives/   "Writing" — articles + reviews + quotes, merged (as now)

  LAYER 2 — KNOWLEDGE GRAPH (open, linked, accretive)
    /reading/    the ledger: what has been read / is being read (as now)
    /notes/      RENAMED + BROADENED "Notebook": quotes AND short notes (§6.1)
    /topics/     NEW: standing subject hubs (§6.4) — the topic map
    /research/   research paths: curated, ordered routes (promote; §6.5)
    /sources/<key>/  per-work node pages (as now; listing stays suppressed)

  SPINE (connects both layers, already built)
    sources: taxonomy  +  tags: taxonomy  +  research paths

The key insight: you don't need new top-level *sections* so much as one new
connective surface (/topics/) and a broadened note model. Everything else is
reframing what exists into two coherent layers so the site can be read either as
a CV-grade professional presence OR as a public knowledge base, depending on
which door the visitor comes through.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CONTENT-TYPE DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 QUOTES -> NOTES (broaden, don't collapse)
  Keep the section. Rename the concept from "quotes/Notebook" to "notes" (public
  label can stay "Notebook"). Support two kinds via a front-matter `kind`:
      kind: quote   — a verbatim passage (current behavior; require `sources:`)
      kind: note    — your own short observation/marginalia (may cite a source,
                      may connect two sources, may stand alone)
  Why: a commonplace book is quotes; a knowledge base is quotes + your thinking
  about them. This is the lowest-friction way to start "thinking in public"
  without every idea needing to become a full article.
  Rule: a `quote` MUST carry `sources:`. A `note` SHOULD carry either `sources:`
  or `topics:` so it can't become an orphan (this is the anti-silo guard).
  Grouping: on /notes/, offer a "by source" and "by topic" view in addition to
  reverse-chron, so the notebook reads as material, not as a microblog.

6.2 REVIEWS (keep the label, unify the data model)
  Do NOT fold reviews into articles. For a historian, a distinct, scannable list
  of book/media reviews is a professional asset (it reads as scholarly output).
  BUT recognize that a review IS a source-anchored article. Unify the data model:
      - a review = section:reviews, REQUIRED `sources:`, optional cover +
        external_url (as today)
      - an article that happens to review something can carry `sources:` too
  Nothing to migrate here — this is mostly a documented principle so the two
  don't drift into different mechanisms. The merged archive already presents them
  together for browsing; the separate section still gives you the clean list.

6.3 SOURCES (leave the mechanism; add a subject facet)
  The per-work page with auto-backlinks is the best thing in the system. Keep it
  exactly. The only addition: let a source declare `topics:` (or reuse tags) so a
  book can appear on a /topics/ hub. Today a source is discoverable by reading
  activity (/reading/) and by inbound links; it is not discoverable by subject.
  Adding a subject facet is what turns the ledger into a library.

6.4 TOPICS (NEW — the highest-leverage addition)
  Create /topics/ as standing subject hubs. A topic is a curated page (like a
  research path, but faceted/standing rather than ordered/linear) that aggregates
  everything on a theme: articles + reviews + notes + sources, with a paragraph
  of editorial framing at the top.
  Two ways to build it, pick per §11:
    (a) PROMOTE TAGS: designate a small set of tags as "topics" and give those
        term pages a richer template (intro prose + grouped sections). Cheapest;
        reuses the taxonomy you already have.
    (b) FIRST-CLASS SECTION: content/topics/<slug>/_index.md with an editorial
        body and either an explicit member list or a tag/`topics` query that
        auto-collects members. More control, more upkeep.
  Recommendation: start with (a) on 3-5 real themes you already have depth in
  (e.g. Indigenous activism; 1990s right-wing movements; rodeo/identity;
  Oklahoma). If a topic wants ordered guidance, that's what a research path is —
  topics and research paths are the standing vs. guided views of the same graph.

6.5 RESEARCH PATHS (promote)
  Keep as-is mechanically. Raise visibility: link them from the homepage "At the
  desk" band and from relevant /topics/ pages. They are the guided counterpart to
  topic hubs and currently under-surfaced.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. NAVIGATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Current: About | Writing | Teaching | Reading | CV

This is good and professional-forward — keep the spirit. The only change the
knowledge layer needs is a door to the graph beyond /reading/. Two options:

  OPTION A (minimal): add one item.
      About | Writing | Teaching | Reading | Notes | CV
    ...where "Notes" (or "Topics") is the knowledge-base front door and links to
    a page that surfaces topics + notes + research paths + the ledger.

  OPTION B (grouped): keep 5 items but let "Reading" become a broader
    knowledge door ("Notebook", "Library", or "Notes") whose landing page fans
    out to reading ledger / notes / topics / research paths, and keep the current
    /reading/ ledger one click in.

Keep the top nav at 5-6 items. Do not surface /sources/, /series/, or raw tag
pages in nav — those are graph-internal, reached by links.

Recommendation: Option A. One clear professional cluster (About/Writing/Teaching
/CV) + two knowledge doors (Reading = activity, Notes/Topics = ideas), which is
exactly the two-layer model made visible in the menu.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WORKFLOW (capture -> connect -> publish -> maintain)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The current draft->publish pipeline (Obsidian vault drafts, newpost.sh,
publish-draft.sh, newsource.sh, finishsource.sh, ship.sh) is excellent and
should not change. The knowledge-base additions are about CAPTURE friction and
GRAPH upkeep, not about the publish path.

  CAPTURE (lower the floor)
    - Add a `note` kind to the quote template so a stray thought is one hotkey,
      not a blank article. Reuse the existing Obsidian Templater quote binding;
      add a sibling "note" template that prefills `kind: note` and prompts for a
      source OR a topic.
    - Keep the rule: nothing gets published without a `sources:` or `topics:`
      handle. Enforce it in preflight.sh (see below). This is the mechanical
      guarantee against re-siloing.

  CONNECT (make the graph the default, not an afterthought)
    - When creating a source (newsource.sh), optionally prompt for one or more
      `topics:` so the book lands on a subject hub immediately.
    - When publishing, if a post's tags include a designated topic tag, it
      auto-joins that hub (if using §6.4a).

  PUBLISH (unchanged)
    - publish-draft.sh handles articles/reviews/notes exactly as today.

  MAINTAIN (new, lightweight)
    - preflight.sh additions:
        * a `quote` with no `sources:` fails (today it's convention only)
        * a `note` with neither `sources:` nor `topics:` fails
        * a `topics:`/topic-tag value with no corresponding hub page warns
    - A periodic (manual) pass to review thin topics and fold or split them.
      Topics are curated, so they need occasional gardening — budget for it the
      same way you already garden research paths.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. PHASED IMPLEMENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do these in order; each phase stands alone and ships value.

  PHASE 1 — Topic map (biggest payoff, least structural risk)
    - Pick 3-5 themes with existing depth.
    - Implement /topics/ via promoted tags (§6.4a): richer term template with
      intro prose + grouped member sections (articles / reviews / notes /
      sources).
    - Add `topics:` (or reuse tags) to existing sources so they appear.
    - Link topics from the homepage "At the desk" band.

  PHASE 2 — Broaden the notebook
    - Add `kind: note` support to the quotes section + templates.
    - Add /notes/ grouping views (by source, by topic).
    - preflight guards for orphan notes/quotes.

  PHASE 3 — Front door + nav
    - Nav: adopt Option A (add Notes/Topics).
    - Build the knowledge-base landing page that fans out to reading / notes /
      topics / research paths.
    - Promote research paths onto that landing + relevant topic pages.

  PHASE 4 — Polish
    - Rename "Notebook" concept to "notes" internally if desired.
    - Reconcile the sources.md "Media Diet" filename collision (§11).
    - Review whether `series/` earns its keep or should be retired.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT TO PRESERVE — DO NOT BREAK THESE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - The sources taxonomy backlink mechanism and citekey folder scheme. It's the
    spine. Everything new hangs off it.
  - The reading feed contract (/reading/index.xml categories + description
    prefixes) consumed by ~/git/micro-theme -> eberle.blog. feed-lint.py guards
    it; keep it green.
  - Reading-event guids (urn:reading-event:...) keyed on isbn/doi/access_url, not
    folder slug. Don't reintroduce slug-based identity.
  - /sources/ staying suppressed (it duplicated /reading/). Topics give sources a
    subject view WITHOUT un-suppressing the raw taxonomy listing.
  - contentSections (articles, reviews, quotes) drives archive/feeds/prev-next.
    If /notes/ stays section:quotes, nothing here changes; if you ever split it,
    update contentSections + static/_headers per docs/workflow.md "Adding a New
    Content Type".
  - The professional-forward homepage and 5-6 item nav. The knowledge layer is
    added AROUND this, not on top of it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR YOU TO DECIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Q1. TOPICS mechanism: promoted tags (§6.4a, cheap, recommended to start) vs a
      first-class content/topics/ section (§6.4b, more control). Start with tags?

  Q2. NAV: Option A (add a 6th "Notes"/"Topics" item) vs Option B (broaden
      "Reading" into a graph door). Recommendation: A.

  Q3. NOTES vs QUOTES: do you actually want to write short original notes in
      public, or is the Notebook deliberately quotes-only? If the latter, skip
      §6.1's `note` kind and just add the topic/source grouping views. This is a
      voice/comfort call only you can make.

  Q4. FILENAME COLLISION: content/sources.md publishes the "Media Diet" page but
      the name reads like the sources/ section. Rename to content/media-diet.md
      (set url or slug to preserve the current path if it's linked/indexed)?

  Q5. SERIES: content/series/ is suppressed and near-empty. Retire it, or is
      there a planned use? Retiring removes a dormant taxonomy.

  Q6. SCOPE OF "OPEN": how open is open? A true digital-garden posture (rough,
      dated-as-evolving, low bar) vs a still-curated-but-linked posture. Your
      revisions/provenance conventions suggest you value polish; topics + notes
      can be as rough or as finished as you set the bar. Decide the bar before
      Phase 2 so the note template's tone matches it.
