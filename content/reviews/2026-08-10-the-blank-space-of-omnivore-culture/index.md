What happened to all the stars? What happened to all that culture that mattered? How did we end up in a world of TikTok stars, manosphere podcasts promoting TikTok stars, and music that caters to the market rather than audiences? Pessimistic questions, but largely ones W. David Marx wants to assess within his book *Blank Space: A Cultural History of the Twenty-First Century*. The book is a chronicle of declining cultural trends brought on by his five horsemen of the cultural apocalypse: omnivorism, poptimism, entrepreneurial heroism, the counter-counterculture, and digital norm evasion.
<!-- more -->
Marx's contention is that over the course of the 21th century, culture has largely been absorbed into this broad monolithic entity seeking to appeal to the most people with broad liberal tendencies. In turn rejection of this omnivore culture has become trendy, contributing to the rise of the alt-right and right-wing movements within the last decade.

Marx's coverage is both expansive and restricted at the same time. He pulls through various ideas throughout the book, most prominently I'd say the rise of streetwear and ultimate embrace by high fashion. Yet at the same time so much of the book became a quick summary of events and ideas, seemingly as a way to show off the wide scope of sources used. As the book moves towards its conclusion there were a number of points where I could see readers getting confused and having questions about the references that aren't contextualized. To pull just one example towards the end of the book while discussing the Zynternet, he also drops a mention of "Barstool conservative." While he defines Zynternet as the term coined for the parts of the internet popular with Zyn nicotine pouch fans, he never defines barstool conservative. Barstool Sports, the site the idea comes from isn't even cited within the book even though it was integral to the formation of that corner of the internet. 

There's more like that but the basic idea is this isn't a particularly in-depth book, but rather an expansive summary. Everyone will probably find references to passing fads during the last quarter-century, but there's not a ton of depth discussion of those ideas. As for the broader pessimism, Marx's remedies at the end of the book boil down to basically be Eddie Vedder and Pearl Jam in the 1990s, but ignore everything they did after 2000.
